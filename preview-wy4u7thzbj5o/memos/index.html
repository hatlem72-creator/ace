Memos

Typed upstairs, dated, and left on the paste-up for anyone who can be bothered.

To: All staffFrom: The Ad ManRe: The ashtrayDate: 12 March 1974

The ashtray in reception is for clients. Staff will use the one by the radio. The plant has been moved two inches. This is not a discussion.

To: The Ad ManFrom: Ellis BrennanRe: LunchDate: 13 March 1974

The union opinion about lunch is that there should be one. The Ace stories will still be here at two o’clock. I have checked.

To: All staffFrom: The Ad ManRe: New York GrooveDate: 4 April 1974

I have been told I hum “New York Groove” until a panel starts to work. This is accurate. It is either inspiring or a workplace issue. We have not voted. Until we do, the humming continues.

To: CreativeFrom: Connie VossRe: Wood veneerDate: 9 May 1974

Wood veneer may be a promise. The promise will run only when the paste-up is square. I have a ruler. I will use it.

To: The Ad ManFrom: ReceptionRe: The toasterDate: 21 June 1974

A man from a small appliance concern asked if we could “give it the Ace treatment.” I said we do not. He left a brochure. It is in the drawer with the other brochures. Debbie has the key.

To: All staffFrom: Chuck Daley’s officeRe: The tieDate: 2 July 1974

Mr. Daley would like it known that the tie is not a joke. The doughnut, however, was for atmosphere, and was eaten off-camera, as agreed.

Est. 1971. New York. World-renowned for the Ace stories. The comics are the masterpiece. The advertisements pay the rent.
