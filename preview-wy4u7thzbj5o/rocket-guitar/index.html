The End… until the next smoke bomb

What really happened
  • The first smoker was low-tech: a smoke bomb in the guitar’s control cavity, lit with a lighter. Concert footage puts its debut between January 1975 (no smoke in San Francisco) and January 1976 (smoke in Detroit).
  • 1976: a second Les Paul became a dedicated smoker. A fake neck pickup hid a halogen bulb, powered through a mic cable from the monitor desk and most likely switched on by a roadie. By early 1977, batteries took over.
  • The heat eventually burned holes through the guitar’s body. A cardboard shield was added to keep the heat off Ace.
  • Rockets: a small headstock firework was used in 1974–75 and dropped by 1976. It came back on the 1979 Dynasty tour as a twin launcher, conceived by Ace and refined by guitar tech Steve Carr, aimed at his smoking guitar hung in the lights. It returned for the 1996–97 Reunion tour.
Ace’s version

These moments come only from Ace’s own interviews. No date or independent report has turned up:

  • The Canadian fireworks shop. Fan researchers can’t line up his “first tour of Canada” with the dates the smoke first appears.
  • The burn: “back in the ’70s” a smoke bomb went off too early inside the guitar and melted his costume to his thigh.
  • The Gene near-miss: he hit the trigger by accident “a couple times”, and one night a rocket flew right past Gene’s head. After that, they built in a safety feature.

Who built it? Ace credits “a designer” for the smoker and “our pyro guy” for the rocket. Fan research names guitar tech Steve Carr for the 1979 launcher.
