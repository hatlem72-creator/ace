Vinyl desk · The interview · 2 Sep 1977

“It Said Fast-Acting”

Darlene Sparks of Upper Fudgeley, Ohio, cleaned her stylus with nail polish remover. It came up spotless. So did the place where the diamond used to be.

By our Vinyl desk · 3 min read

Darlene’s apartment looks like a magazine spread, and smells like a salon. She opens the door chewing gum, and does not stop.

Darlene, what did you do to your turntable?

I cleaned the needle. Nail polish remover, on a cotton ball. *pop* It’s what I use on anything that needs to look new.

Why remover?

The bottle said “gentle” and “fast-acting”. One of those was true. *pop*

What happened?

The remover dissolved the glue. The diamond left. I played side one of everything with a tiny metal stump. It doesn’t follow the grooves any more. It digs new ones.

And the records themselves?

I was generous with the cotton ball. The vinyl went cloudy and soft. Then the edges went wavy. *pop*

“It doesn’t follow the grooves any more. It digs new ones.”

Do they still play?

They play. Everything sounds like it’s being sung from inside a can of hairspray. My brother says Fleetwood Mac sounds better. I’ve stopped speaking to my brother.

What does the apartment smell like?

A salon. My neighbour came over yesterday to book a perm. *pop*

So you’re buying them back?

Every Saturday at Vern’s. There’s a queue now. It’s mostly me and a man who glued his. *pop* Together we smell like a strip mall.

Is that… a date?

It’s a queue. *pop* We’ve had coffee twice. He brought his own mug. It was stuck to a record.

Any advice for our readers?

Support your local record shop. And keep the remover for your nails. *pop* That’s what it’s for.

Fine print: Darlene is invented. Never put nail polish remover on a stylus or a record. Clean a stylus with a soft brush, dry, from back to front. No records were harmed in the making of this interview. Two hundred and six were harmed before it.
