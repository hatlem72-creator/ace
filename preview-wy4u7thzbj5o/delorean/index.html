Gone in Fifth Gear!

The End… of his driving, for a while

What really happened
  • Saturday 21 May 1983, White Plains, NY: a “minor accident” near Bank and Main Streets.
  • Police found him arguing with the other driver. He took off at up to 90 mph, south and then north on the Bronx River Parkway, and drove around a roadblock.
  • He hit four cars, went the wrong way on a one-way street and struck a railroad bridge abutment. Nobody was hurt.
  • About 20 minutes later a witness saw him leave the battered car less than a mile from the first crash. He was arrested at a flower shop where he had stopped to use the phone.
  • Charged with drunken driving, reckless driving and leaving the scene. He posted $500 bail on Monday.
  • August 1983: fined $600, license suspended for six months, and a one-year conditional discharge with counselling.
Ace’s version

Retelling it decades later, Ace remembered a few things differently from the 1983 press reports:

  • He says he was driving on a suspended license with no registration, which is why he ran.
  • He calls the first bump a fender-bender worth about $250, and says he told the cop “okay, no problem” before telling his friend to get out and taking off.
  • He says he drove “80 miles an hour against traffic” and the police Chevy Nova “couldn’t keep up”.
  • He remembers a deli phone booth and walking out to “like 15 cops with their guns drawn”. The reports say flower shop, and don’t mention drawn guns.

The chase lived on in song: “Rock Soldiers” on Frehley’s Comet (1987) is partly about that day.
